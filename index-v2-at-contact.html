PromptED

Empowering Media Professionals against Disinformation

Company
European Institute of Participatory Media
Role
Lead designer & researcher
Main Contributions
Concept, System, UI/UX & User Research
Team
Computer Scientist x2, Design Consultant

PromptED is an interactive simulator that helps media professionals understand the principles of generative AI and their associated risks, to encourage responsible AI use within the journalistic workflow.

Understanding AI Use within Journalism

To understand media professionals' perceptions towards generative AI, a series of 18 semi-structured interviews were conducted. The results were combined with an extensive literature review and served as the basis for our design direction.

Using Ethics as a Foundation

PromptED is an interactive simulator aimed at building appropriate mental models for journalists using experiential learning techniques. To ensure responsible use for trustworthy content production, it was key to base learnings on existing ethical guidelines.

Ethical Learning Foundations

Distilling Learnings & System Design

Using existing journalistic workflows established in the interviews and the ethical foundations, a system of learnings for journalists was established. First, journalists explore the guided simulator, where they learn key principles, and next they apply these learnings with their own prompts in the playground.

Modules Breakdown

Users can navigate a set path or select learnings of their own choosing. This allows users of varying knowledge to navigate PromptED appropriately.

Reducing Visual Strain

In addition to the system design, I was responsible for design of the individual flows and features. A key challenge within the prompt playground was how to show changes between original and optimised output. It was decided to use a two-column system where past text was displayed next to new.

  • Two texts are displayed in one column: underlined blue text is new/replaced text, and red strikethrough is the old. Users can expand or switch out sentences to compare the two texts.
  • Red box icons show text that was in the previous output that does not have a counterpart.
  • Old output text is separated into another column for direct comparison.
  • Direct lines connect old and new output sentence equivalents.

Testing & Co-Creation

Throughout the entire project, co-creation sessions, workshops and user tests were leveraged to guide the design. In total, over 10 co-creation and workshop sessions were conducted, 9 individual usability tests were run, and an SUS score of 78.86 was achieved in preliminary testing alone. Users highlighted helpfulness and a match with real-world needs.

Outcome

As an EU project, PromptED has the potential to be rolled out and aid journalists across the EU. Early indications have shown improvement in mental models already, and therefore help reduce the amount of misinformation entering our media.

People's Toilet Builder

The world's first standardised compendium for toilet design

Company
Royal College of Art, Imperial College London
Responsibilities
Concept, System, User Research & Experience
Team
Innovation Manager, Design Researcher, Creative Director

The People's Public Toilet Builder is a user-centric digital platform aiming to aid and promote good public sanitation systems. Machine learning and crowd-sourced information are leveraged to assist professionals like architects in making informed decisions when planning toilet projects.

Research | A Need for Pooled Information

3.4 billion people lack access to clean, safe and accessible sanitation services. Local leaders and officials do not have enough information to build sanitation systems.

Building a Global Picture

Interviews with various stakeholders in the value chain were conducted across Asia, North America and Africa. User journeys were then mapped and feedback incorporated into our vision.

  • Professor of Architecture, Illinois, USA
  • Sanitation Development, Bangalore, India
  • Sanitation Department, Kaduna State Government, Nigeria

Current compendiums, use-cases and standards were analysed to better understand requirements for the project and what "makes a successful project."

Lo-fi to Hi-fi Prototypes

The flow was designed to ensure user-centred design decisions would be baked into the building process. The report and questions were catered to the user's role — for example, an architect may get design guidelines, and a project officer, legislation.

Prototypes were constructed and used to gain feedback from users and other stakeholders in the process. This was useful in keeping the project aligned to its goals.

Outcome

The People's Public Toilet Builder had been taken forward to the implementation stage with the aid of the Gates Foundation as well as SuSanA and GIZ collaborators, however has since been stopped. This project would have been the world's first comprehensive toilet compendium and has great value to the hygiene and safety of millions across the world.

Pod

Monitoring Symptoms of Chronic Fatigue Syndrome

Company
Royal College of Art, Imperial College London, Pratt Institute
Responsibilities
Physical Computing, Product Service System Design, Research
Team
Solo Project

Pod is a medical device using ECG technology to help track chronic fatigue syndrome. By using novel electrode "tattoos," Pod generates continuous medical-grade readings with minimal discomfort to the user.

Research | HMW Monitor Chronic Fatigue?

Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS) is a disease that can stop people from carrying out basic functions due to extreme exhaustion. Tracking symptoms is key in battling CFS, but current methods are tiring and inaccurate.

Developing the Form and Function

Prototyping was split into making a works-like and looks-like prototype separately. The looks-like was guided by the works-like, considering size and dimensions, how it fit on the body, and using medically safe materials such as silicone and skin tapes.

The physical prototype was worn to stress-test the design and ensure it could be worn for days at a time. Surprisingly, it even held up in the shower!

Electrode Lead Design

The process of ECG placement was studied and showed that electrodes are placed relative to a point on the chest. This is reflected in the design with the central notch.

Dr. James Gill, 2019 (How to perform an ECG)

Proof of Concept

To make the electrode "tattoo" possible, it was hypothesised that conductive ink could be used as electrode material. Using this material means the leads could have a fixed shape that was flexible to the body.

A 3-point ECG was created using Arduino, and the results from a normal electrode, an electrode with conductive ink, and a control without leads at all were recorded. The test showed first signs of proof of concept.

Design Outcome

Feature Set

  1. Silicone Casing
  2. NMC Li-ion Battery
  3. Microcontroller
  4. ECG Module
  5. Haptic Motor

How it works

  1. Users fill out measurements to get fitted electrode "tattoos"
  2. Align and adhere the electrodes to your body
  3. Slot the adhesive over the pod
  4. Adhere the pod to the electrode point
  5. A haptic sensor alerts the user when they are over-exerting themselves

Lafayette Gardens

Increasing Access to Resources in Social Housing Developments

Company
Royal College of Art, Imperial College London, Pratt Institute
Responsibilities
Facilitation, Information Design
Team
Solo Project

Lafayette Gardens is a community-led project within a social housing development in Brooklyn, New York. "A home to be proud of" was created as a logbook and guide to help residents better access resources available to them. Created by the residents, for the residents.

Exploring Social Housing in New York City

Lafayette Gardens is a residential complex in Brooklyn with a large aging population of 384. With the help of Mr Ty, the head of the residents association, I started exploring and integrating myself into the community to better understand their needs.

Key Insights

  • Many residents find it difficult to transition to the new digital social housing systems
  • Mr Ty, head of the residents association, is the main source of help for NYCHA-related problems
  • Residents feel grateful and can trust Mr Ty's word, not the city's

Designing Together

Three official co-design workshops were held with distinct themes. To prepare for each workshop, a clear but flexible agenda was set and example ideas were created to help with creativity. Food and drinks were provided.

Minimal but Meaningful

  • Named after a previous booklet, for older residents to recognise
  • Easy to flip to sections without need for special printing
  • Designated space to place important documents
  • A note grid was created in the shape of the Lafayette building

Distributed to the Residents

The Lafayette Gardens logbook has been handed over and is currently under production to be given to over 800 residents, more than 350 of which are seniors.
